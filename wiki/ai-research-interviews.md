# AI Research Interviews

## The numbers

- **11 companies, 57 formal interviews**, plus **46 recruiter calls** and **16 post-offer chats**, and many informal networking conversations.
- 4 offers before accepting OpenAI.
- She described the process as genuinely grueling ("stressed and miserable" at points) despite an excellent CV — struggle is the norm, not a signal you're failing.

## Key insight

**Technical skill is weighed far more than research experience — but research is what gets you in the door.** Companies spend most of the interview loop evaluating technical depth and knowledge, not your publication record. Having a recognizable area of expertise (for Alisa: tokenization) helps you stand out.

## Interview types encountered

- **ML coding** — by far the most common round.
- **General coding** — LeetCode-style rounds.
- **Rapid-fire technical Q&A** — e.g. 5D parallelism, positional encodings.
- **Research discussions** — your work and research taste.
- **Math assessments** — from fun logic puzzles to serious pen-and-paper derivations (probability, linear algebra, calculus).
- **Behavioral** rounds.

## Preparation resources (what she actually used)

1. **[Stanford CS336 — Language Modeling from Scratch](https://stanford-cs336.github.io/)** — watched end to end for breadth and a coherent mental picture of the field.
2. **CS336 Assignment 1 (assignment1-basics)** — implementing and debugging a transformer from scratch; the single highest-ROI drill because it shows up constantly. Turn it into muscle memory.
3. **Her own shared notes:**
   - *Alisa's Book of LLMs* (Notion, `alisawuffles.notion.site`) — neural-net basics → mathy things → the modern transformer LM (architecture, FLOPs accounting, inference memory).
   - *Alisa's math notes* — probability, linear algebra, calculus refreshers.
4. **LeetCode 75** and **Neetcode Blind 75** — the two canonical lists for general-coding rounds.
5. **Deep dives** one concept at a time via blogs, papers, chatting with ChatGPT/Claude, and implementing from scratch. Specific reads she recommended:
   - Self-Attention & Transformers (Stanford CS224n notes)
   - The Illustrated GPT-2 (Jay Alammar)
   - Backpropagation (CS231n notes)
   - Introduction to Policy Gradients for LMs

## Strategy advice

- **Referrals matter.** Sometimes you need someone inside the company vouching for you to get through the door.
- **Interview order:** conventional wisdom says warm up on companies you care less about and save first choices until you already hold offers. Alisa's caveat: don't overdo practice runs — "by the time you get to the companies you actually care about, you'll already be burnt out."
- **Timelines aren't under your control.** Whether a company has headcount, and which team is actively hiring, often matters more than your preparation level. Get firsthand intel from friends inside companies and from recruiters instead of guessing.
- **Manage energy deliberately** — plan the calendar, expect emotional swings, avoid burnout.

## Post-offer phase

The work isn't done when offers arrive. Expect an extended period of learning about your options and negotiating: conversations with prospective teammates and managers, lunch visits, recruiter calls. She recommends **pre-scripting negotiation conversations** after receiving an offer.
