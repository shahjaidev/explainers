MiMo-V2.6: three dials of RL

A seven-minute, 3Blue1Brown-style walkthrough of Xiaomi's Scaling Reinforcement Learning Towards Self-Improvement report and the live training dashboard at mimo.xiaomi.com/rl. It covers one run that scaled RL compute, environment diversity and grader compute together, and the tricks that kept it stable.

7 min · 1080p · captioned, no audiorendered with Manim

    The key points

    The same ideas as the video, in text. Each card matches a chapter.

    01Only mixed groups teach

    GRPO scores each of 16 attempts against its own group. If all 16 pass or all 16 fail, every advantage is zero and the compute is wasted.

    A dynamic sampler drops those groups (about 15% all-fail and 23% all-pass on the dashboard) and keeps pulling until it has 1,568 prompts that carry signal.

    02One step is huge

    1,568 prompts × 16 rollouts is about 25K trajectories, or 2.7–3.7B tokens per update, with contexts up to 1M tokens.

    Pro's compute went 43.8% to rollouts, 43.5% to training and 12.7% to grading. The run cost about $2.6M for Pro and $0.9M for Flash.

    03Never wait for the slowest rollout

    Training is fully asynchronous: new weights are pushed into the inference engines mid-rollout. Each token records the policy version that produced it.

    A trajectory is admitted only if enough of its tokens are fresh, and the stale prefix is masked from the loss. Per-token importance ratios are bounded on both sides.

    04You Only RL Once

    There are no per-domain specialists to merge. Every batch mixes 50 datasets: about two-thirds code, then visual, general agent, cybersecurity and chat.

    Many lightweight harnesses (system prompt, tools, context management) are trained in the same batch. The gap to held-out harnesses narrows during RL.

    05Groupwise agentic grading

    Pass/fail can't tell a clean fix from a flailing one that also passes. An agentic grader reads the whole group side by side.

    GRS writes a task-specific rubric from the contrasts. GAR moves advantage toward the shorter, better passing solutions without changing the total. Pass rate keeps rising while turn counts stay flat.

    06Stability at a trillion parameters

    With a trainable MoE router, expert load drifted within about 20 steps: CV went from 0.78 to 2.0, the busiest expert from 6× to 16× the mean, and cold experts from 0.5% to 22%. The fix was to freeze the router.

    Reward hacking is handled by several layers: reward design, adversarial evaluation, anomaly detection and cross-verified validators.

    07It kept paying off

    DeepSWE avg@3 rose steadily with cumulative RL cost: Pro went from 58.4 to 72.6 and Flash from 48.7 to 65.7.

    Pro (1.02T total, 42B active) scored 46 on the Artificial Analysis index, the top open-weights model at launch. Flash is 309B total with 15B active. Both are MIT-licensed.

    Re-render it

    The source is mimo-v26-rl/explainer.py, a single Manim Community scene. It needs no LaTeX and uses the CMU (Computer Modern) fonts.

    pip install manim            # 0.21; needs pango/cairo dev libs
apt install fonts-cmu
manim -qh mimo-v26-rl/explainer.py MiMoRL
