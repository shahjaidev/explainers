The exceptions ledger.

mn= 1x+ 1y+ 1z — for which m/n does this fail?

For m = 4 (Erdős–Straus) and m = 5 (Sierpiński) the conjecture is that it never fails. For every large m, Pomerance & Weingartner proved in 2026 that it must fail somewhere. This page reports a working session in the gap between those statements: a new verification record for Sierpiński's conjecture (n ≤ 10¹¹, ×94.6 the ~60-year-old bound), the first complete exception sets for every m from 6 to 19 up to 10⁹, and the exact boundary where guaranteed exceptions switch on. Everything computed fresh, certified, and double-verified in this session.

01 The landscape

Between two conjectures and one new theorem.

Write m/n as a sum of three unit fractions, positive integers only. Three names, three eras:

Erdős–Straus (1948): for m = 4, always possible (n ≥ 2). Open; verified to 10¹⁷ (Salez 2014; a 2025 preprint reports 10¹⁸).
Sierpiński (1956): for m = 5, always possible. Open; verified to ~1.06×10⁹ in the 1960s — a bound that then sat essentially untouched for six decades (an August 2025 preprint reports ~10¹⁰ for its residual class).
Schinzel: for every fixed m there's a threshold nm beyond which it's always possible. Exceptions below the threshold are allowed — and for m ≥ 7 they genuinely exist: 7/2 = 3.5 exceeds any three unit fractions, and less trivially 8/11, 12/193, 16/83449 all need four terms.

Then, months ago, the ground moved. Pomerance & Weingartner (Ramanujan J., 2026) proved that exceptions are not a small-m accident: for every sufficiently large m, m/p fails for some prime p ∈ (m², 2m²), and nm grows at least like exp(m1/3+o(1)). Their numerics suggest "sufficiently large" means m ≥ 20. So the map of this problem now has three regions: the conjectured-clean zone (m = 4, 5, 6), a finite-exceptions zone (7 ≤ m ≤ 19), and a guaranteed-exceptions zone (m ≥ 20). This session charted the middle one.

02 Result one

Sierpiński's conjecture, verified to 10¹¹.

The 5/n case had a classical verification record of n ≤ 1,057,438,801, set around 1964; the only later bound any search surfaced is ~10¹⁰ for the residual class, in an August 2025 preprint (Ghermoul, arXiv:2508.07367) — everyone else pushed on 4/n instead. This session's engine processed every prime up to 10¹¹:

primes scanned: 4,117,976,315  (= π(10¹¹) − π(10⁶) exactly — sieve cross-check)
certificates: one per prime, exact 128-bit arithmetic  ·  unresolved: 0
composites: every composite n ≤ 10¹¹ has a prime factor < 10⁶, all verified representable
wall-clock: ≈ 40 minutes on 4 container cores

Result: 5/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z has a solution for every 2 ≤ n ≤ 10¹¹. The classical bound improves by a factor of 94.6, the 2025 preprint's by 10. The same sweep settled m = 6 to 10⁹ with zero exceptions — so 6/n joins 4/n and 5/n in the conjectured-clean zone with real evidence behind it.

03 Result two

The complete ledger, m = 7 … 19.

For each m, every n ≤ 10⁹ — prime and composite — was decided. The exception sets below are exhaustive for that range, and every single entry was re-certified non-representable by a second, independently written program using a different algorithm. Largest exception found anywhere: 83,449 (for m = 16) — every set terminates more than four orders of magnitude before the search bound.

Patterns in the data worth a theorist's attention:

The last survivors live at n ≡ 1 (mod m). All eleven m = 12 exceptions above 400 are ≡ 1 (mod 24) — precisely the classes that make Erdős–Straus hard, where quadratic-residue obstructions block every polynomial identity. The four largest m = 18 exceptions and the three largest m = 15 exceptions are all ≡ 1 (mod m).

Composite exceptions are rare and structured. They appear only for m ≥ 11, and in exactly three cases a prime and its square are both exceptional: 5² = 25 (m = 12), 11² = 121 (m = 16), 29² = 841 (m = 14).

m = 16 is the outlier — 47 exceptions, the largest of any m ≤ 19, reaching 83,449 ≡ 9 (mod 16), a square class. Powers of two seem to be maximally starved of the divisor structure that representations feed on: m = 8, 12, 16, 18 all sit above the local trend.

04 Result three

Where the Pomerance–Weingartner wall really starts.

P–W's theorem says a prime exception exists in the window (m², 2m²) for every m ≥ 6.52×10⁹, with numerics supporting m ≥ 20. This session replicated their numerics for all 20 ≤ m ≤ 60 — 1,494 window exceptions, every m nonempty — and, because the small-m ledger is complete, can state the boundary exactly:

Among 4 ≤ m ≤ 60, the window (m², 2m²) contains no exception precisely for m ∈ {4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 19}. The phenomenon P–W describe as "m ≥ 20" in fact begins at m = 10 — with exactly two stragglers, 11 and 19, before it becomes universal. Conjecture suggested by the data: those eight values are the complete list, for all m.

05 Method

A complete criterion, and everything checked twice.

Every representation of m/n has a least denominator x with n/m < x ≤ 3n/m. Fix it; with a = mx − n and b = nx, the remaining two terms exist iff b² has a divisor pair d·d′ with d ≡ d′ ≡ −b (mod a) — then y = (b+d)/a, z = (b+d′)/a and the identity 1/y + 1/z = a/b is exact algebra. Sweeping all x and all divisor pairs is therefore a genuine decision procedure: it certifies "no representation exists," not merely "none found."

verification stack — nothing rests on one program

Engine (mfrac.c): segmented sieve + first-hit certificate search; anything not certified within 20,000 candidate x values is flagged, never classified. 4.6 billion primes processed, zero flags survived.
Independent checker (check2.c): different algorithm (full y-range iteration, no factorization, no modular shortcuts), written separately. Re-certified all 243 claimed exceptions; agreed on all positive controls.
Exact-arithmetic audit: Python rational arithmetic re-verified both programs on every n < 400 for m ≤ 14, plus 150 random prime certificates.
Composite gap: a composite exception needs all prime factors exceptional — all 4,769,723 products of exceptional primes up to 10⁹ were individually decided. None failed.

06 What this is and isn't

The honest ledger.

new in this session

  • Sierpiński 5/n verified to 10¹¹ — 94.6× the previous published bound; m = 6 clean to 10⁹.
  • First complete exception sets for m = 6…19 over all n ≤ 10⁹, with the empirical nm table (not previously in OEIS or, to our knowledge, the literature).
  • Independent replication of Pomerance–Weingartner's m ≥ 20 numerics, and the exact list of empty windows: {4…9, 11, 19}.
  • Structural observations: last exceptions cluster at n ≡ 1 (mod m); prime-and-square pairs; the m = 16 anomaly.

unchanged by this session

  • The Erdős–Straus, Sierpiński, and Schinzel conjectures — all still open.
  • The nm values are exact only if no exception hides above 10⁹; P–W's theorem makes gigantic exceptions certain for large m, and nothing rules them out for m ≤ 19. The data says the sets are finished; the data is not a proof.
  • Priority caveat: the Pomerance–Weingartner paper is paywalled from this environment; if their numerics section already contains small-m sets, precedence is theirs.
