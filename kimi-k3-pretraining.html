The claim

2.5× scaling efficiency

The architectural, data and training changes in K3 don't just make the model better at a fixed budget — they move the whole loss-versus-FLOPs curve. On held-out out-of-distribution validation data, K3's fitted curve sits to the left of K2's by a factor of roughly 2.5 in compute.

That framing matters. It isn't "K3 scores higher than K2" — K3 is nearly three times the size, so it had better. It's that a K3-family model reaches any given loss with 2.5× less compute than a K2-family model. The architecture changes earn their complexity budget.

Because these changes also move the optimal training regime, Moonshot re-ran dedicated scaling-law studies rather than inheriting K2's hyperparameters. Retuned: batch size, learning rate, tokens-per-parameter ratio (TPP), and model shape.

A methodological aside worth reading

Cosine beat WSD — but only under a fair comparison

Prior work has reported that Warmup-Stable-Decay matches or beats cosine decay. K3's study found the opposite, and the reason it gives is a good piece of experimental hygiene.

The two schedules have markedly different optimal hyperparameters. Even at identical model size and token budget, their best peak learning rates and batch sizes differ substantially. So comparing them on a shared hyperparameter grid doesn't compare schedules — it compares how well each schedule happens to suit hyperparameters that were tuned for one of them.

Why a shared grid rigs the result

illustrative — the argument, not the data

Under an independent scaling-law search for each schedule, cosine decay consistently reached lower final loss. K3 therefore uses cosine as default, with a 1% linear warmup.

Rephrasing: getting more from a finite corpus

Following K2's recipe, knowledge and mathematics corpora are rephrased with style- and perspective-diverse prompting, generated chunk-wise autoregressively, and then verified for fidelity against the source document. The third step is the one that keeps this from being a hallucination pipeline: a rephrasing that drifts from its source doesn't ship.

The vision corpus, and one deliberate bet

The taxonomy follows K2.5: captions, interleaved image–text documents, OCR, perception, video, and visual coding data, combining open collections with in-house filtering, synthesis and deduplication.

Two choices stand out. First, coordinate supervision is provided in both absolute and normalized [0,1] formats — so localization is both precise and robust to resolution, which is exactly what a model that clicks around GUIs and reads charts needs.

Second, and more consequential: K3 substantially scales up programmatic multimodal data, pairing code snippets with their rendered visuals across SVG, 3D assets, webpages, games and CAD schematics. This is the pre-training substrate for the write-code → look-at-the-render → fix-it loop. It is also, almost certainly, why K3 tops the WebDev Arena and wins 3D/WebGL/Shader tasks by a +59-point margin in blind expert judging.

The programmatic multimodal corpus is the quiet link between a pre-training decision and a post-training result. You cannot RL a model into vision-in-the-loop coding if it never saw code paired with its own output.

Optimization

Training recipe

Native multimodal from step zero. Language and vision are optimized jointly from the start — no vision encoder grafted on afterwards, no post-hoc alignment stage. Visual and textual tokens interleave inside a single next-token-prediction objective, so the shared backbone learns unified representations from the outset.

Optimizer detail

Per-Head Muon

Muon orthogonalizes the momentum matrix via Newton–Schulz iterations before applying it. K3 refines this for attention projections: instead of orthogonalizing the whole Q, K, V matrix, it partitions the momentum along the head dimension and orthogonalizes each head's block separately.

The reasoning is a scale argument. Full-matrix orthogonalization treats all heads as one coupled block, so heads carrying larger gradient or momentum magnitudes dominate the shared update direction, and quieter heads get updates that were never properly normalized for them. Per-head orthogonalization equalizes update scale across heads.

One block, or many

12 heads with unequal momentum scale

It also happens to be cheaper: Newton–Schulz on tall per-head blocks costs less than on the full projection matrix. A rare case where the principled choice is also the faster one.

Reach

Getting to a million tokens

No positional encoding to extend

The cheapest part of K3's long-context story is the part that isn't there. With NoPE, position is carried implicitly through KDA's recurrent gating and decay. There is no RoPE frequency base to retune, no YaRN interpolation to fit, no positional surgery at all — the model extrapolates to 1M directly.

Length is not capability

Natural long documents and videos are full of junk: near-duplicates, binary blobs, truncated files, throwaway clips, machine-generated logs. K3 runs them through exact and fuzzy deduplication, perceptual hashing over video frames, heuristic and classifier quality filtering, and structural validation. Because genuinely long, coherent sources are scarce relative to short text, they are then upsampled so the long-context distribution isn't drowned out during cooldown.

But the report is blunt that this still isn't enough: "Length alone does not confer long-range capability." A model trained on long documents can happily learn to attend locally and ignore the rest. So K3 synthesizes long-context data — permuting and concatenating multimodal documents and sub-tasks so the embedded tasks are only solvable by attending to information scattered across the full window. That trains attention at the intended scale instead of letting it degenerate into local patterns.

Four-stage context curriculum

click a stage

The economics are the point: long-sequence computation is expensive per token, so concentrating it in a small fraction of the total budget keeps the curriculum affordable while still letting the model adapt gradually to longer dependencies. The sequence-dimension partitioning that makes million-token KDA training tractable at all — KDA Context Parallelism — is an infrastructure story, covered in Part 01.

Modality, from the data side

The vision tower was trained from scratch

Prior practice — including K2.5 — initializes the vision encoder from a contrastively pre-trained model such as SigLIP, on the premise that pre-existing visual knowledge gives a head start. K3 abandons that, and the stated reason is training stability, not accuracy: under joint optimization the SigLIP-initialized tower showed persistently higher gradient norms with frequent spikes, while the from-scratch MoonViT-V2 stayed smooth throughout.

There's a second, more interesting argument. Training the encoder with next-token prediction lets the language-modeling objective shape its representations directly, rather than a contrastive loss that favours global semantics over the fine-grained textual and structural cues a document-reading, chart-parsing, screenshot-inspecting model actually needs.

And the result was a wash on quality: MoonViT-V2 matched the SigLIP-initialized baseline across vision evaluations. Which turns a stability fix into a general claim — contrastive pre-training is unnecessary as an initialization for multimodal LLMs at scale.

Summary

What to take away

  • The headline is a curve shift, not a score. 2.5× scaling efficiency over K2 on held-out OOD validation — architecture, data and recipe together.
  • Changing the architecture invalidates the old hyperparameters. K3 re-ran scaling-law studies for batch size, LR, TPP and model shape rather than inheriting K2's.
  • Schedule comparisons need per-schedule tuning. Cosine beat WSD only once each was given its own optimum; a shared grid would have decided the question by accident.
  • Programmatic multimodal data is a strategic asset. Code paired with its rendered output — SVG, 3D, webpages, games, CAD — is the pre-training root of K3's strongest post-training results.
  • NoPE makes long context nearly free to extend. No RoPE retuning, no interpolation; position lives in KDA's decay.
  • Long data must be synthesized, not just collected. Filtering and upsampling handle quality; only deliberately scattered tasks force attention to actually use the window.
  • Contrastive vision initialization was dropped, and nothing broke. A from-scratch next-token-prediction tower was more stable and equally good.
